# Ten-step method

This workflow combines multi-perspective evidence gathering, curriculum design, retrieval practice, Feynman explanation, and compression. It is inspired by the ten-step learning method described by Daliu, but these operational instructions are independently written.

## Global invariants

- Treat the ten steps as a dependency graph, not ten unrelated essays.
- Separate sourced facts from synthesis and recommendations.
- Calibrate depth to the learner profile.
- Prefer concrete examples, counterexamples, decisions, and exercises over definitions alone.
- For each step, produce input/upstream, full artifact, and three to five learning takeaways.

## 1. Five-perspective inquiry

Investigate the topic through five deliberately different lenses:

1. Practitioner: operational realities, tacit knowledge, and common implementation failures.
2. Researcher: strongest peer-reviewed or otherwise rigorous evidence, including disagreement with popular belief.
3. Skeptic: strongest counterargument and evidence the mainstream may discount.
4. Economist: incentives, costs, beneficiaries, market structure, and conflicts of interest.
5. Historian: relevant precedents, recurring patterns, and how comparable cases ended.

For each lens, give a concise position, its strongest traceable evidence, one distinctive insight, and source links. Do not force symmetry when the evidence is asymmetric.

## 2. Contradiction map

Consume all five perspectives. Identify direct conflicts, the premise behind each conflict, relative evidence strength, the question that would resolve the largest disagreement, shared ground, and an omitted blind spot. Make uncertainty explicit.

## 3. Integrated brief

Consume steps 1 and 2. Produce a sixty-second executive explanation, five findings ordered by reliability, supporters and dissenters for each finding, one cross-perspective connection, one role-specific action, and one frontier question whose answer could materially change the conclusion.

## 4. Peer-review audit

Consume step 3. Score each finding from 1 to 10 for reliability and explain the score. Name the least certain conclusion, the evidence needed to test it, any over-weighted perspective, a missing sixth perspective, and the most important revision a demanding domain reviewer would request. Avoid prestige role-play; judge against explicit evidence and reasoning criteria.

## 5. Resource curation

Use current evidence tools to verify five real learning resources. Mix formats only when useful. For each resource record why it earns a place, how to use it, time required, the one idea or capability to extract, and a canonical link. Add realistic traps and arrange the five resources into a one-week path. Do not recommend a resource solely because it is popular.

## 6. Learning ladder

Consume the topic, profile, and step 3. Define five levels from complete beginner to confident practitioner. For every level include: stage name, required understanding, observable mastery, focus concepts or skills, promotion milestone, hands-on exercise, common error, and a self-test. Estimate the learner's current rung cautiously and state the next milestone.

## 7. Core 20 percent

Consume steps 3 and 6. Select the small set of concepts and skills that unlock most practical progress, and explain the selection logic. Build ten micro-lessons that fit roughly two focused hours. Each lesson needs an objective, key idea, exercise, optional resource, expected result, and five retrieval questions. End with a small project that demonstrates use rather than recognition.

## 8. Static retrieval-practice bank

Consume steps 3, 6, and 7. Create ten questions with increasing difficulty: three introductory, three intermediate, two advanced, and two expert. Put each answer behind a disclosure control in HTML. Include a high-quality answer and a 0-10 rubric for every question, then add five answer-free challenge questions. Highlight misconceptions the questions are designed to expose.

The separate `$ten-step-08-adaptive-exam` skill provides the live, one-question-at-a-time version.

## 9. Static Feynman explanation

Consume the central concepts from step 3. Explain each selected concept in three layers: plain language and an everyday example, three to five likely points of confusion with targeted clarification, and a simple but precise final definition. Include one boundary or counterexample so simplicity does not become inaccuracy.

The separate `$ten-step-09-feynman-loop` skill provides the live explain-diagnose-reteach version.

## 10. Five-minute field sheet

Consume all prior steps. Compress the topic into a plain-language definition, short high-value rules or steps, three to five realistic examples, common mistakes, a before-use checklist, and five quick questions. Optimize for recall before a meeting, interview, project, or explanation.

## Completion check

Confirm that all ten steps exist, every step names its upstream inputs, steps 1 and 5 are sourced or explicitly marked offline, steps 8 and 9 are static materials, and the final sheet reflects conclusions that survived step 4.
